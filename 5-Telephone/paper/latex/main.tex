\begin{abstract}
Generative-agent work shows that information can \emph{spread} through a simulated
society, but not whether the spread is \emph{faithful}. We introduce \textsc{Telephone},
an instrument that injects a single ground-truthed update into an LLM-agent society,
lets the agents relay it through rounds of pairwise conversation, and then interviews
every agent to measure what the society \emph{holds}. Across capability, connectivity,
memory architecture, and a persistent authoritative source, natural levers fail to
restore held truth; only directly overwriting every agent's memory does. The sharpest
result is a dissociation: an authoritative source makes agents increasingly \emph{say}
the current value while the society's \emph{held} belief stays near baseline---speech
moves without belief. We trace the mechanism to path-dependent entrenchment: the version
established early and broadly wins, and late or narrow correction can be uttered without
being adopted. The effect survives a long horizon (truth peaks early then decays), three
scenarios, thicker personas, and a semantic-judge validation of the metric.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% TODO: tighten. Frame: reach vs fidelity; individual vs collective fidelity.
Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly talk to one another: they coordinate,
remember, and simulate social environments \citep{park2023generative}. A separate line of
work shows that a single agent can be made remarkably faithful to a real individual
\citep{park2024thousand}. Neither line asks the question we study here: when a society of
such agents passes a \emph{changing} fact through conversation, does the society end up
\emph{holding} the truth, or merely repeating it?

This is the gap between \emph{reach} and \emph{fidelity}. Misinformation research has long
separated how far content spreads from whether it is believed
\citep{lazer2018science,vosoughi2018spread}. We bring that distinction inside an LLM-agent
society and make it measurable. Our contributions are: (i) a measurement shift from
information reach to information fidelity; (ii) a robust phenomenon of social fidelity
decay; (iii) an empirical spine showing natural repair levers fail; (iv) a speech--belief
dissociation with a path-dependent entrenchment mechanism; and (v) a rigor story of powered
reruns, semantic-judge validation, multiple scenarios, and explicit retractions of early
non-replicated claims.

\section{The Telephone Instrument}
\label{sec:method}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig1_setup.png}
\caption{The \textsc{Telephone} instrument. A ground-truthed update is injected at one
source; agents relay it over $K$ rounds of pairwise meetings using generative-agent
reflection memory; every agent is then interviewed and scored as holding the
\emph{current}, \emph{stale}, or \emph{unknown} value. The paper hinges on two distinct
measurements: what agents \emph{say} in meetings versus what the society \emph{holds} at
interview.}
\label{fig:setup}
\end{figure*}

A society of $N{=}25$ LLM agents is seeded so that one source carries a ground-truthed
update (e.g., a community repair drive moved from Saturday to Sunday). Agents meet in
pairs over several rounds; each agent reads and relays through generative-agent reflection
memory \citep{park2023generative,shinn2023reflexion}. At the end we interview every agent
with a probe (``when/where is it now?'') and score the answer as \emph{current},
\emph{stale}, or \emph{unknown} (Figure~\ref{fig:setup}). We separate \emph{speech}
(current-value utterances in meetings) from \emph{held belief} (the interview answer).

\section{Results}

\subsection{The society does not preserve a ground-truthed update}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig2_decay.png}
\caption{Long-horizon fidelity decay. Held current-truth rises early (peak near round~6)
then erodes toward a low steady state; an authoritative source decays too, converging to
baseline, while only continuous broadcast sustains truth. A short-horizon snapshot at
round~5 therefore slightly overstates long-run retention.}
\label{fig:decay}
\end{figure}
The current value reaches the conversation stream but does not reliably become the
society's held answer. Over a long horizon (Figure~\ref{fig:decay}) truth can rise
transiently---peaking near round~6---then decays toward a low steady state by round~30.
The important object is the trajectory: truth can appear and still lose.

\subsection{Natural levers do not solve the failure}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig3_failed_levers.png}
\caption{Failed levers. Capability gives only a modest bump and stays far below the
broadcast cure (99\%); connectivity is roughly neutral; swapping in a currency-resolving
memory is not significant. Error bars are SEM.}
\label{fig:levers}
\end{figure*}
Each lever is an intuitive repair story, and each fails (Figure~\ref{fig:levers}).
Capability helps modestly but leaves most agents off the current truth; connectivity is
roughly neutral; a currency-resolving memory does not reliably rescue truth; a persistent
authoritative source changes speech but not held belief; only broadcasting the truth to
every agent every round works---an overwrite-style positive control, not a social cure.
The claim is not that agents cannot output the truth; they can. Ordinary social
communication does not install it as later held belief.

\subsection{Speech and belief dissociate}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig4_dissociation.png}
\caption{Speech $\neq$ belief. A persistent authoritative source raises what agents
\emph{say} (final-round current utterances, 54$\to$75\%) while what the society
\emph{holds} stays near baseline (12$\to$15\%). Broadcast moves both.}
\label{fig:dissoc}
\end{figure}
This is the centerpiece (Figure~\ref{fig:dissoc}). Under a persistent source, agents
increasingly utter the current value, yet final interviews remain near baseline; broadcast
to all moves both speech and belief. We describe ``held belief'' operationally: the answer
an agent gives when later probed---we make no claim about literal mental states.

\subsection{The mechanism is entrenchment, not recency}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig5_mechanism.png}
\caption{Entrenchment, not recency. A single \emph{early} all-agent broadcast succeeds
(100\%); a \emph{late} broadcast immediately before the probe fails (9\%). What governs
held belief is whether the truth becomes the early, broad version that later conversation
reinforces.}
\label{fig:mech}
\end{figure}
If recency drove belief, a late all-agent broadcast just before the probe should work; if
repetition drove it, a single early broadcast should fail. The opposite happens
(Figure~\ref{fig:mech}): early succeeds, late fails. The stale value begins with incumbent
advantage; narrow or late correction can be uttered without taking over the society's held
state \citep{thorson2016belief,kirby2008cumulative}.

\subsection{Generality and measurement checks}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/figS1_generality.png}
\caption{Robustness. The baseline/source/broadcast pattern holds across three scenarios and
under thicker, self-report-style personas: broadcast restores truth ($\sim$99\%) while the
authoritative source never approaches the cure.}
\label{fig:general}
\end{figure*}
The pattern replicates across \textsc{repair\_drive}, \textsc{book\_club}, and
\textsc{carpool}, and persists under thicker personas (Figure~\ref{fig:general}),
addressing the concern that the effect is a thin-persona artifact
\citep{park2024thousand}. A semantic LLM judge agrees with the keyword metric on the
headline (99--100\%), so the current/stale verdict is not a keyword artifact.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Agent societies and communicative agents.}
Generative agents establish a setting in which LLM agents talk, remember, and coordinate
in a believable social world \citep{park2023generative}, and communicative-agent systems
make agent-to-agent dialogue a standard application substrate
\citep{li2023camel,wu2023autogen}. Social-evaluation environments broaden assessment beyond
isolated question answering \citep{zhou2024sotopia}, and recent work shows a \emph{single}
agent can be made faithful to a real individual \citep{park2024thousand}. We use this
substrate to ask a narrower reliability question that these systems do not: whether a
\emph{changing} fact remains current after social transmission---collective rather than
individual fidelity.

\paragraph{Reach versus fidelity in (mis)information.}
Misinformation research separates how far content spreads from whether it is believed and
acted upon \citep{lazer2018science,vosoughi2018spread,friggeri2014rumor}. LLM-agent
simulations now reproduce misinformation propagation through social networks
\citep{maurya2025simulating}, but they measure propagation or vulnerability. \textsc{Telephone}
shifts the endpoint from \emph{reach} to \emph{held-belief fidelity} after a ground-truthed
change, asking what the society later answers rather than how far a message travels.

\paragraph{Factuality, debate, and the speech--belief gap.}
Factuality evaluation treats truth as a semantic behavioral target rather than fluency
\citep{lin2022truthfulqa,manakul2023selfcheckgpt}. Multi-agent debate is the optimistic
counterpoint---structured discussion can improve judged answers
\citep{du2023improving}---yet explicit correction can fail without reliable grounding
\citep{huang2024selfcorrect}. We identify a complementary, society-level failure: the
correct statement can win space in the conversation without becoming the answer agents
later hold.

\paragraph{Memory, persistence, and recursive degradation.}
Agent-memory and reflection methods show that durable state must be engineered---exposure
in context is not the same as retrievable belief \citep{shinn2023reflexion}. On the human
side, corrected misinformation continues to shape attitudes \citep{thorson2016belief}, and
iterated-learning experiments show that serial transmission converges toward attractors
\citep{kirby2008cumulative}---precedents for our entrenchment account. Finally,
model-collapse work provides a training-time analogy for recursive degradation
\citep{shumailov2023curse,alemohammad2024selfconsuming}; \textsc{Telephone} studies a
communication-time analogue that arises without any retraining.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% TODO. Honest boundaries.
We do not claim a connectivity/capability phase boundary, that connectivity amplifies
corruption (an early underpowered cell did not replicate), or that memory is a cure. We
claim that ordinary social transmission does not reliably install a changing fact as held
belief, and that the governing factor is early/broad entrenchment. Limitations include the
single base task family, the scale of the society, and the operational definition of held
belief.

\section{Conclusion}
In LLM-agent societies, a society can learn to \emph{say} the truth without coming to
\emph{hold} it. The dissociation is driven by path-dependent entrenchment, survives a long
horizon and several robustness checks, and resists every natural repair lever short of
overwriting memory directly.
