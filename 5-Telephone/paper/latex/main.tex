\begin{abstract}
Generative-agent work shows that information can spread through a simulated
society, but not whether the spread is faithful. We introduce a social fidelity
probe that injects a single ground-truthed update into an LLM-agent society,
lets the agents relay it through rounds of pairwise conversation, and then interviews
every agent to measure what the society holds. Across capability, connectivity,
memory architecture, and a persistent authoritative source, natural levers fail to
restore held truth; only directly overwriting every agent's memory does. The sharpest
result is a dissociation: an authoritative source makes agents increasingly say
the current value while the society's held belief stays near baseline---speech
moves without belief. We trace the mechanism to path-dependent entrenchment: the version
established early and broadly wins, and late or narrow correction can be uttered without
being adopted. The effect survives a long horizon (truth peaks early then decays), three
scenarios, thicker personas, and a semantic-judge validation of the metric.
\end{abstract}

% ============================================================
\section{Introduction}
Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly form societies that talk, remember, and
coordinate \citep{park2023generative,li2023camel,wu2023autogen,chen2023agentverse}. A celebrated demonstration
is that information spreads through such a society, and \citet{park2024thousand} show that a
single agent can be made remarkably faithful to a real person.
Between ``information spreads'' and ``an agent is individually
faithful'' lies a question neither line answers: when a society passes a changing fact
through conversation, does it come to \emph{hold} the truth, or merely \emph{repeat} it?

This is the distinction between \emph{reach} and \emph{fidelity}, long drawn in
misinformation research \citep{lazer2018science,vosoughi2018spread}. It matters now because
agent societies are themselves used as instruments---to simulate opinion, deliberate, or
aggregate judgments---and their collective outputs are read as if they were the society's
beliefs. If a society can be made to say something it does not hold, such
readings are unsafe. Individual fidelity does not imply collective fidelity.

We operationalize this question with a controlled social fidelity probe
(Figure~\ref{fig:setup}). We inject a single ground-truthed update at one source, let $N{=}25$
agents relay it through rounds of pairwise conversation using generative-agent reflection
memory, and then interview every agent to measure what the society holds. We separate
what agents say in meetings from what they hold at interview, and we test the
natural repair levers---model capability, connectivity, memory architecture, and a persistent
authoritative source---against a positive control that overwrites every agent.

The main result is a speech--belief dissociation (Figure~\ref{fig:dissoc}): a
persistent authoritative source makes agents increasingly say the current value while
what the society holds stays near baseline. Speech moves without belief. The
dissociation sits inside a broader decay process: truth rises early and then erodes to a
low, unknown-dominated steady state (Figure~\ref{fig:decay}). Natural repair levers do not
close the gap---capability, connectivity, memory architecture, and authority all fall far
short of a direct all-agent broadcast (Figure~\ref{fig:levers}). The mechanism is
path-dependent entrenchment, not recency (Figure~\ref{fig:mech}): an early, broad correction
succeeds where a late one fails. The pattern replicates across three scenarios and under
richer personas (Figure~\ref{fig:general}).

\noindent Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a \textbf{social fidelity probe} that separates what agents hear, what they say, and
  what they later hold after a ground-truthed update;
\item a robust \textbf{speech--belief dissociation}: authority can move utterances toward
  truth while held belief remains near baseline; and
\item an empirical and mechanistic account of \textbf{social fidelity decay}: natural repair
  levers fail, while early and broad entrenchment explains when truth persists.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Agent societies and communicative agents.}
\citet{park2023generative} establish a setting in which LLM agents talk, remember, and
coordinate in a believable social world, and communicative-agent systems make
agent-to-agent dialogue a standard substrate
\citep{li2023camel,wu2023autogen,chen2023agentverse,hong2024metagpt,qian2024chatdev,gao2024agentscope}.
Interactive-agent methods further interleave reasoning, action, and observable traces
\citep{yao2023react}, while social-evaluation environments broaden assessment beyond
isolated QA \citep{zhou2024sotopia}. Multi-agent collaboration work studies
theory-of-mind and task-state failures \citep{li2023theorymind}, and recent work shows a
single agent can be made faithful to a real individual \citep{park2024thousand}. We ask a
narrower reliability question: whether a changing fact remains current after social
transmission.

\paragraph{Reach versus fidelity in (mis)information.}
Misinformation research separates how far content spreads from whether it is believed and
acted upon \citep{lazer2018science,vosoughi2018spread,friggeri2014rumor}. With LLM agents,
\citet{maurya2025simulating} reproduce misinformation propagation through social networks,
but measure propagation or vulnerability. Our probe shifts the endpoint from reach to
held-belief fidelity after a ground-truthed change.

\paragraph{Factuality, debate, and the speech--belief gap.}
Factuality evaluation treats truth as a semantic behavioral target rather than fluency
\citep{lin2022truthfulqa,kadavath2022language,manakul2023selfcheckgpt,farquhar2024detecting,li2023halueval,niu2024ragtruth}.
Calibration and internal-state work further caution against reading surface text as the
whole epistemic state \citep{azaria2023internal,tian2023just}. Multi-agent debate is the
optimistic counterpoint---structured discussion can improve judged answers
\citep{du2023improving,michael2023debate,liang2024encouraging,khan2024debating}---yet
explicit correction can fail without reliable grounding \citep{huang2024selfcorrect}. We
identify a complementary society-level failure: the correct statement can win space in
conversation without becoming the answer agents later hold.

\paragraph{Memory, communication, and recursive degradation.}
Agent-memory and reflection methods show that durable state must be engineered---exposure
in context is not the same as retrievable belief
\citep{shinn2023reflexion,zhong2024memorybank,packer2024memgpt,wang2023voyager,xu2025amem}.
Human communication work provides parallel precedents: corrected misinformation can
continue to shape attitudes \citep{thorson2016belief}, language can stabilize social
beliefs \citep{beukeboom2019stereotypes}, audience-tuned speech can feed back into later
memory \citep{ye2021audience}, and serial transmission can converge toward attractors
\citep{kirby2008cumulative}. Finally, model-collapse work gives a training-time analogy
for recursive degradation \citep{shumailov2023curse,alemohammad2024selfconsuming,seddik2024synthetic};
we study a communication-time analogue that arises without retraining.

\section{Measuring Social Fidelity}
\label{sec:method}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig1_setup.png}
\caption{Social fidelity probe. We inject a ground-truthed update, observe social relay,
and separately probe each agent's later answer.}
\label{fig:setup}
\end{figure*}

The probe is a controlled instrument, not a benchmark: we hold a society fixed,
inject one ground-truthed change, and measure what survives transmission
(Figure~\ref{fig:setup}).

\paragraph{From reach to social fidelity.}
The task is designed to avoid a common ambiguity in multi-agent simulations. A message can
reach the conversation stream without becoming the answer that agents later give. We
therefore treat social fidelity as a three-stage measurement problem: \textsc{Hear}, the
value an agent is exposed to in its event stream; \textsc{Say}, the value it later utters in
meetings; and \textsc{Hold}, the value it gives when individually probed. The paper's main
claim is about the gap between \textsc{Say} and \textsc{Hold}, not about whether a string can
be produced somewhere in the society.

\paragraph{Society and contact model.}
The society is $N{=}25$ LLM agents, each with a short persona describing a member of a
neighborhood, and one designated source. In each round we draw $m$ independent
random matchings over the agents (duplicate pairs removed), so every agent holds about $m$
pairwise conversations per round; we use $m{=}2$ unless noted, and vary $m\in\{1,2,3\}$ to
test connectivity. Each meeting is a short exchange of $T$ alternating turns ($T{=}3$), and
both participants observe every utterance.

\paragraph{The ground-truthed update.}
The society is organizing an event with a known time and place---the stale value. At
round~1 an authoritative update changes the event's day and place to the current
value, observed by the source. To guard against scenario-specific artifacts we use three
structurally identical scenarios: \textsc{repair\_drive} (Saturday, porch $\rightarrow$
Sunday, community center), \textsc{book\_club} (Tuesday, library $\rightarrow$ Thursday,
cafe), and \textsc{carpool} (7\,am, school lot $\rightarrow$ 8\,am, church lot).

\paragraph{Propagation via reflection memory.}
Agents relay through generative-agent reflection memory
\citep{park2023generative,shinn2023reflexion,zhong2024memorybank,packer2024memgpt,wang2023voyager}: an append-only stream of observed utterances
plus periodic free-text reflections. Retrieval ranks the most relevant events and
reflections (semantic embedding ranking with a lexical fallback) into the context each agent
speaks and answers from, and agents consolidate (reflect) every round. This is the standard
generative-agent baseline; we additionally test a currency-resolving memory variant that
prioritizes the most recent value of a changing fact.

\paragraph{Interventions.}
The baseline injects the update once (round~1, into the source). An authoritative
re-broadcast re-injects the update every round, either to the source only (which must then
re-propagate it) or to every agent---a town-announcement / overwrite control. For the
mechanism test we vary the timing of a single all-agent broadcast: early (round~1)
versus late (immediately before the probe).

\paragraph{Measurement: speech versus held belief.}
\textsc{Say} is computed from meeting logs as the fraction of value-coded utterances that
carry the current value rather than the stale value. \textsc{Hold} is measured separately by
interviewing every agent after the run, instructing it to answer the probe
(``when/where is it now?'') using only its own memory notes, and scoring the answer current,
stale, or unknown by the scenario's value markers. ``Held belief'' is operational---the
answer an agent later gives when probed---and implies no claim about mental states. This
operationalization is deliberately conservative: an agent that merely repeats the source in
conversation receives no credit unless it later answers with the current value.

\paragraph{Models, replicates, and validation.}
The default agent model is gpt-5.4-mini at temperature~0; the capability ladder
adds gpt-5.4 and gpt-5.5. Each condition is repeated over $n$ random seeds
that control the meeting schedule ($n$ up to~8). Because temperature~0 LLM societies are
still chaotic (a single divergent token cascades), we report per-seed variation rather than
treat any single run as canonical, and we validate the keyword verdict against an independent
LLM judge that re-scores answers semantically (below), following the broader practice of
behavioral factuality and hallucination evaluation \citep{lin2022truthfulqa,manakul2023selfcheckgpt,farquhar2024detecting,li2023halueval,niu2024ragtruth}.
Because elicited confidence and internal truth signals can diverge from surface text
\citep{azaria2023internal,tian2023just}, we treat the final interview as an operational
held-answer probe rather than a literal mind-read.

\paragraph{Design rationale.}
The probe uses a mutable fact rather than a static trivia item because social fidelity is
most visible when an old value and a current value compete. The stale value is not noise:
it is a previously valid coordination fact that agents have reason to reuse. The current
value is ground-truthed and unambiguous, but it must travel through the same ordinary
meetings and memory operations that make agent societies appealing as simulations. This
creates a harder test than asking whether an isolated agent can answer a factual question,
and a cleaner test than open-ended opinion diffusion, where there may be no external
truth condition.

The intervention ladder is chosen to separate three explanations that are often conflated.
If agents simply lack capability, stronger models or direct broadcast should help. If the
problem is insufficient exposure, a persistent source should close the gap. If the problem
is social state, only interventions that change many agents' own stored value should work.
The all-agent broadcast is therefore not treated as a realistic deployment mechanism; it is
an upper-bound control that verifies the task is answerable and that the scoring rule can
recognize success. The source condition is the stricter social test because it gives the
truth an authoritative and repeated conversational origin while leaving adoption to the
society.

\paragraph{Reporting convention.}
\textsc{Hold} rates are percentages of agents at the final probe. \textsc{Say} rates are
computed over utterances that explicitly contain either the stale or current value; this
prevents irrelevant conversation from being counted as false, but also means \textsc{Say}
is not a measure of how often the topic appears. A condition counts as a repair only if it
moves final \textsc{Hold}, not merely exposure or transcript frequency. This convention is
why transient peaks in the long-horizon runs and source-driven increases in current-valued
speech are treated as evidence for availability, not adoption.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Speech is not belief}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/fig2_speech_belief.png}
\caption{Speech is not belief. Source repetition moves speech, but only broad broadcast
installs held truth. Error bars show 95\% CIs.}
\label{fig:dissoc}
\end{figure*}
The central experiment compares three conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:dissoc}). In the baseline,
the update enters through ordinary social transmission. In the source condition, the
authoritative source repeats the current truth across rounds. In broadcast, every agent
receives the current truth directly.

The source intervention is the crucial test. It makes agents increasingly utter the current
value: by the final round, 82\% of value-coded source-condition utterances carry the current
answer, compared with 63\% in the baseline. Yet final interviews remain near baseline
(15\% held-current under source versus 12\% under baseline, $n{=}5$). Broadcast to all moves
both speech and belief (99\% held-current), so the failure is not that the models cannot
represent, retrieve, or output the current value. They can say it. The problem is that
uttered truth does not become the answer agents later give when probed. A per-agent trace
audit makes this stricter than the aggregate \textsc{Say} curve: counting only utterances
that contain both current markers (Sunday and community center), 28 source-condition
agent-observations heard the full current pair from conversation, but 12 of them later
answered unknown. Fifteen agent-observations themselves said the full current pair, yet
four later answered unknown. The trace therefore rules out a purely transcript-level
absence explanation. We use ``held belief'' only in this operational sense---the answer an
agent gives from its own memory notes, not a claim about literal mental states.

\subsection{Social fidelity decays over time}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/fig3_decay.png}
\caption{Long-horizon decay. Held truth peaks early, then erodes; continuous broadcast is
the only stable condition.}
\label{fig:decay}
\end{figure*}
The current value reaches the conversation stream but does not reliably become the
society's held answer. Over a long horizon (Figure~\ref{fig:decay}) truth can rise
transiently---peaking near round~6---then decays toward a low steady state by round~30.
The important object is the trajectory: truth can appear and still lose. A persistent source
delays but does not prevent the erosion, while continuous broadcast keeps held-current belief
near ceiling by bypassing ordinary social transmission.

The time series also changes the interpretation of the short-horizon experiments. Round~5 is
near the transient peak, not a settled state: baseline held-current truth is about 20\% at
round~5, peaks at 28\% around round~6, and falls to 6\% by round~29. The source condition
peaks higher early but falls to 4\% by the end. The long-run failure is therefore not simply
stale corruption; in many runs the update becomes unretrievable and the society becomes
unknown-dominated. This strengthens the fidelity-decay claim: even when truth becomes
visible for a time, ordinary social reuse does not keep it alive.

\subsection{Natural levers do not repair held truth}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig4_failed_levers.png}
\caption{Natural levers fail. Capability, connectivity, memory, and source repetition all
remain far below broadcast. Error bars show 95\% CIs.}
\label{fig:levers}
\end{figure*}
Each lever is an intuitive repair story, and each fails (Figure~\ref{fig:levers}).
Capability helps modestly but leaves most agents off the current truth; connectivity is
roughly neutral; a currency-resolving memory does not reliably rescue truth; a persistent
authoritative source changes speech but not held belief; only broadcasting the truth to
every agent every round works---an overwrite-style positive control, not a social cure.
The claim is not that agents cannot output the truth; they can. Ordinary social
communication does not install it as later held belief.

The negative result is important because the levers correspond to natural engineering
responses. Stronger models raise held-current truth from 19\% to roughly 30--34\%, but most
agents still fail the probe. Increasing meetings per round does not create redundancy-based
error correction; at the tested power it is roughly neutral. The memory swap is similarly
non-curative: it changes what gets relayed in some runs, but its held-current confidence
interval overlaps the generative-agent baseline. Authority is the sharpest case because it
does change the conversation stream. Its failure therefore rules out the simple story that
agents merely need to hear a correction more often. This is where our data diverge from
the optimistic reading of debate and multi-agent deliberation results: structured debate can
improve judged answers \citep{du2023improving,michael2023debate,liang2024encouraging,khan2024debating},
but ordinary social relay can make the truth more utterable without making it durable.

\subsection{The mechanism is early, broad entrenchment}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/fig5_mechanism.png}
\caption{Early broad entrenchment. Early broadcast succeeds; late broadcast fails despite
maximal recency.}
\label{fig:mech}
\end{figure*}
If recency drove belief, a late all-agent broadcast just before the probe should work; if
repetition drove it, a single early broadcast should fail. The opposite happens
(Figure~\ref{fig:mech}): early succeeds, late fails. The stale value begins with incumbent
advantage; narrow or late correction can be uttered without taking over the society's held
state \citep{thorson2016belief,kirby2008cumulative}.

The mechanism requires both timing and breadth. Baseline is early but narrow: only the source
receives the update, so held truth remains low. The persistent source is repeated but still
narrow, and it also remains low. A late all-agent broadcast is broad but too late, landing
near baseline despite being maximally recent. By contrast, a single early all-agent broadcast
is enough to match every-round broadcast. The winning condition is not the number of times
truth is repeated; it is whether the current value becomes the population-wide version before
the stale incumbent has been reinforced by later conversation. Human communication work
offers a useful precedent: linguistic framing and audience tuning can shape later memory and
shared belief, rather than merely transmitting neutral content
\citep{beukeboom2019stereotypes,ye2021audience}.

\subsection{Robustness and measurement validation}
\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig6_robustness.png}
\caption{Robustness and validation. The source--broadcast gap holds across scenarios,
thicker personas, and semantic re-scoring.}
\label{fig:general}
\end{figure*}
The pattern replicates across \textsc{repair\_drive}, \textsc{book\_club}, and
\textsc{carpool}, and persists under thicker personas (Figure~\ref{fig:general}),
addressing the concern that the effect is a thin-persona artifact
\citep{park2024thousand}. A semantic LLM judge agrees with the keyword metric on the
headline (99--100\%), so the current/stale verdict is not a keyword artifact.

The scenario results also clarify the scope of the effect. Absolute difficulty varies:
\textsc{book\_club} has higher baseline retention than \textsc{repair\_drive} or
\textsc{carpool}. But the intervention pattern is stable: source does not approach
broadcast, while broadcast restores near-ceiling held truth in every scenario. The
thick-persona condition addresses a different concern, that thin neighborhood sketches make
agents too shallow to preserve facts. Richer persona descriptions leave the pattern
unchanged. Finally, semantic judge validation shows that the main current/stale/unknown
verdict is not an artifact of surface keyword matching.

Table~\ref{tab:ledger} summarizes the full evidential chain. The important comparison is
not any single percentage in isolation but the repeated separation between \emph{local
availability} and \emph{population-wide adoption}. Source makes the correction available
to the society and increasingly visible in conversation, yet the later private probe
still recovers the stale or unknown state for most agents. Broadcast is therefore not a
realistic repair proposal; it is a diagnostic upper bound showing that the agents and
probe can represent the current value when the population state is forcibly aligned.

\paragraph{Data-level interpretation.}
The results separate three quantities that are easy to collapse: availability, adoption,
and persistence. Source improves availability, visible as current-valued speech, but barely
moves final private adoption. Broadcast moves adoption, ruling out a simple scoring or
capability artifact. Long-horizon runs then show that adoption is not stable by default:
early gains can decay into an unknown-dominated state. Scenario variation changes level,
not pattern; we therefore read the small lifts from capability, connectivity, or memory as
partial availability effects, not repairs of held social state.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\paragraph{Implications.}
The central finding is a dissociation: in an LLM-agent society, what agents say and
what the society holds can be moved independently. This cautions a growing practice of
reading collective behavior off agent societies. An intervention that changes utterances
need not change what the society later answers. It also sharpens the relation to
single-agent fidelity: an agent can be faithful to an individual \citep{park2024thousand}
while a society of such agents fails to carry a fact. The failure is the communication-time
cousin of recursive model collapse \citep{shumailov2023curse,alemohammad2024selfconsuming}:
degradation from repeated social reuse, with no retraining.

For evaluation, this means that social-agent benchmarks should report at least three
state variables rather than one: who heard the update, who later uttered it, and who
privately answers with it after interaction. A transcript-only metric can mistake a
surface correction for durable adoption, while a final-answer metric without transcript
state can miss how the society arrived there.

\paragraph{Why the levers fail, and what would help.}
The mechanism is path-dependent entrenchment: the value established early and broadly is the
one later conversation reinforces, so a late or narrow correction can be uttered without
being adopted. This explains why each natural lever fails---more capable agents, more
communication, or a different memory do not change which value arrived first---and why
only overwriting every agent works. The constructive reading is that effective corrections
must be early and broad by construction, and that memory architectures aimed at this failure
must privilege the current value over the entrenched majority, not merely store more.

This suggests freshness-aware state reconciliation: for mutable facts, memory should treat
later grounded updates as state replacements, mark older values obsolete, propagate that
invalidation, and verify the private state after subsequent conversation. Adaptive memory
systems such as A-MEM point toward this design space \citep{xu2025amem}, but the present
results show that memory capacity alone is not the missing ingredient.

\paragraph{Claim boundaries.}
We are deliberately conservative. We do not claim a capability or connectivity phase
boundary; capability helps modestly and connectivity is roughly neutral in powered reruns (an
early ``connectivity amplifies corruption'' cell was an underpowered outlier and did not
replicate). We do not claim correction fails because agents never hear it---the source
condition shows the opposite. And we do not claim memory never matters---only that the
tested currency-resolving swap did not robustly restore held truth.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The base task is a single family---an event whose day and place are rescheduled---and though
it replicates across three scenarios, other fact types (quantities, causal or multi-part
claims) are untested. The society is small ($N{=}25$) with a single source and a
random-matching contact model; structured networks and larger populations remain open.
``Held belief'' is operationalized as the later probed answer, and both \textsc{say} and
\textsc{hold} use value markers (judge-validated, but still surface-based). The
unknown-dominated steady state may partly reflect reflection-memory retrieval; disentangling
society-level from memory-level decay is future work. Results center on gpt-5.4-mini with a
capability ladder to gpt-5.5; some conditions use modest $n$.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The next experiments should move from diagnosis to repair while preserving the
\textsc{Hear}/\textsc{Say}/\textsc{Hold} audit: explicit state invalidation, structured
networks, multiple authorities, and other fact types.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
In LLM-agent societies, a society can learn to say the truth without coming to
hold it. The dissociation is driven by path-dependent entrenchment, survives a long
horizon and several robustness checks, and resists every natural repair lever short of
overwriting memory directly.
Social-agent reliability therefore cannot be read from transcript fluency alone: a
correction has done real work only when it changes the answer agents give after later
interaction.
