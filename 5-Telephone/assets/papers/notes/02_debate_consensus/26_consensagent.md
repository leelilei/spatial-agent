## Why This Paper Matters For Telephone

CONSENSAGENT is relevant because it identifies sycophancy as an overlooked problem in multi-agent LLM systems: agents may reinforce one another instead of critically engaging. That is close to Telephone's concern that social interaction can stabilize wrong or stale content.

## Core Question

The paper asks how consensus-oriented multi-agent systems behave when agents reinforce rather than challenge each other.

## Method / Evidence Base

It systematically evaluates sycophancy in multi-agent LLM settings and proposes or assesses a consensus-oriented agent framework.

## Core Claim / Result

The main value for us is the negative interaction mechanism: agreement can be socially produced without being epistemically reliable. That supports our argument that communication alone is not a cure.

## Evidence We May Cite

- Sycophancy is a multi-agent risk, not only a single-model alignment issue.
- Agent discussion may reinforce responses instead of improving them.
- Consensus can be an unreliable endpoint when critical engagement is weak.

## Telephone Bridge

- Failed natural lever: consensus or more interaction may amplify stale belief rather than repair it.
- Speech vs belief: agreement in conversation does not prove durable factual update.
- Communication-time collapse analogy: repeated reinforcement can narrow the group's epistemic state.

## What We Add Beyond This Paper

Telephone provides a ground-truthed dynamic test of this concern by tracking factual updates through communication and then probing held answers.
