## Why This Paper Matters For Telephone

This paper is important for the speech/belief framing because it suggests model internal states can contain truthfulness information not obvious from surface text. Telephone cannot access internals, but our behavioral split points in the same direction: utterance is not the whole epistemic story.

## Core Question

The paper asks whether internal activations can reveal whether statements are true or whether a model is generating false information.

## Method / Evidence Base

It trains classifiers on model internal states for statements supplied to or generated by the model, evaluating whether truthfulness can be decoded.

## Core Claim / Result

The relevant result is that truthfulness information may be present in internal representations. This supports being careful about the distinction between produced text and latent state.

## Evidence We May Cite

- Internal model states can carry signals related to truthfulness.
- Surface confidence or fluency is not the only evidence available about factual status.
- The paper helps justify our cautious language around held belief as an operational measure.

## Telephone Bridge

- Speech vs belief: direct support for not equating utterance with internal epistemic state.
- Measurement / rigor: Telephone uses behavioral probes because internal probes are model-specific.
- Fidelity vs reach: a message can be correct while the receiver's later state remains stale.

## What We Add Beyond This Paper

Telephone studies this distinction socially and behaviorally rather than through activation probes.
