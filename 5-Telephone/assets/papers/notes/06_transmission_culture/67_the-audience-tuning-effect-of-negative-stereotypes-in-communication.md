## Why This Paper Matters For Telephone

Audience-tuning is one of the best human communication analogues for our speech-belief split. It studies how adapting a message to an audience can later bias the communicator's own memory or evaluation of the target.

## Core Question

The paper asks how tuning negative stereotype-related messages to an audience affects later memory, impressions, or belief about the target.

## Method / Evidence Base

It uses social-psychological communication experiments where speakers tailor descriptions for an audience and are later tested on their own recall or impressions.

## Core Claim / Result

The relevant finding is the audience-tuning effect: communicative choices made for social reasons can feed back into the speaker's own cognition. This is a human precedent for utterance shaping later belief.

## Evidence We May Cite

- Audience-directed communication can bias later memory or impressions.
- Socially tuned messages are not merely external performances; they can have internal cognitive consequences.
- The paper provides a direct bridge between speech acts and later held representations.

## Telephone Bridge

- Speech vs belief: what agents say in a social setting may later affect what they hold.
- Entrenchment / path dependence: early audience-tuned formulations can bias later state.
- Fidelity vs reach: communication goals can distort content beyond simple transmission.
