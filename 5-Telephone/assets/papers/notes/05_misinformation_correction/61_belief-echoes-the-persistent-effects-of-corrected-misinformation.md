# Belief Echoes: The Persistent Effects of Corrected Misinformation

## Why This Paper Matters For Telephone

This is a strong mechanism-side reference for the idea that correction can be
accepted while the effect of misinformation persists. The paper's key phrase,
"belief echoes," is almost perfectly aligned with the conceptual space around our
source condition: agents can be exposed to corrective information, and even say
the current truth, while the stale version still shapes what the society finally
holds.

We should use it as a human misinformation analog, not as direct evidence about
LLM agents. It gives us established language for why correction does not imply
erasure.

## Core Claim / Result

The paper argues that negative political misinformation can continue shaping
attitudes even after the misinformation is successfully discredited. A correction,
even when believed and delivered immediately, does not necessarily eliminate the
effect of the initial misinformation. The paper distinguishes affective belief
echoes from cognitive belief echoes, with motivated reasoning making some echoes
more likely when misinformation fits prior views.

## Evidence We May Cite

- Corrected misinformation can retain downstream effects.
- Believing a correction is not equivalent to undoing the influence of the false
  information.
- Immediate correction is still insufficient in some cases, which is highly
  relevant to our finding that late broadcast is no better than baseline.
- Persistence can arise through different mechanisms: affective carryover or
  cognitive reasoning from the mere presence of the claim.

## Telephone Bridge

- Fidelity vs reach: A correction reaching the recipient is not enough; its effect
  on downstream belief/attitude is the relevant endpoint.
- Speech vs belief: The human analog is believing or accepting a correction while
  attitudes remain affected. Telephone's agent analog is saying the correction
  while held belief remains stale.
- Entrenchment / path dependence: Supports the idea that first exposure has a
  lasting footprint. Telephone operationalizes this as early/broad social
  entrenchment.
- Failed natural lever: Provides a precedent for why fact-checking/source
  correction may fail to erase prior influence.
- Measurement / rigor: Encourages measuring final belief-like endpoints rather
  than merely whether a correction was seen or accepted.

## What We Add Beyond This Paper

Belief Echoes is about human political attitudes after corrected misinformation.
Telephone is about LLM-agent societies and a controlled ground-truthed update.
Our added mechanism is population-level timing and breadth: early broadcast to all
agents succeeds, late broadcast to all agents fails, and a persistent source
changes speech without significantly changing held belief.

## Draft-Ready Use Sentence

> Human misinformation research has shown that corrections can be accepted while
> the effects of the false claim persist; Telephone finds an analogous social
> failure in LLM-agent societies, where corrective speech can circulate without
> dislodging the entrenched held version.
