# CAMEL: Communicative Agents for “Mind” Exploration of LLM Society

## Why This Paper Matters For Telephone

CAMEL is one of the standard early references for autonomous communicative agents. It matters because Telephone depends on the premise that multi-agent conversation is now a normal experimental and application substrate.

## Core Question

The paper addresses how to reduce human steering by letting role-playing LLM agents cooperate autonomously through conversation.

## Method / Evidence Base

It uses inception prompting and role assignment to create communicative agents that decompose and solve tasks through dialogue.

## Core Claim / Result

The contribution is a scalable pattern for autonomous agent-agent cooperation. It demonstrates the plausibility of LLM societies but does not evaluate whether factual updates remain stable across social transmission.

## Evidence We May Cite

- Role-conditioned LLM agents can collaborate through natural-language interaction.
- Conversation itself is treated as the coordination medium.
- The paper helps establish that autonomous agent societies are not just speculative.

## Telephone Bridge

- Fidelity vs reach: CAMEL shows coordination; Telephone measures fidelity of what coordination transmits.
- Speech vs belief: CAMEL evaluates task progress through conversation; Telephone probes later held answers.
- Measurement / rigor: it motivates why communication logs alone are insufficient for our question.

## What We Add Beyond This Paper

Telephone adds a controlled factual-update task, social transmission rounds, and post-conversation belief probes. It turns communicative-agent interaction from a capability demo into a fidelity stress test.
