## Why This Paper Matters For Telephone

This paper is relevant because it treats multi-agent collaboration as requiring models of other agents' mental states. Telephone's belief probes make a complementary point: we need to distinguish what agents say socially from what they appear to hold internally.

## Core Question

The paper evaluates whether LLM-based agents show theory-of-mind-like abilities in cooperative multi-agent tasks.

## Method / Evidence Base

It uses a cooperative text-game setting with ToM inference demands and compares LLM agents against reinforcement-learning or planning baselines.

## Core Claim / Result

The paper suggests that LLM agents can show emergent collaborative or ToM-like behavior in some settings but still face limits. For Telephone, the relevance is the focus on latent beliefs and partner modeling.

## Evidence We May Cite

- Multi-agent LLM collaboration can require reasoning about other agents' beliefs or intentions.
- Interactive tasks expose capacities not visible in isolated prompting.
- The paper helps motivate belief/state probes in agent societies.

## Telephone Bridge

- Speech vs belief: ToM work reminds us that utterances and internal state are different objects.
- Fidelity vs reach: collaboration success does not guarantee accurate belief updating.
- Measurement / rigor: Telephone operationalizes held belief through post-transmission queries.

## What We Add Beyond This Paper

Telephone is not testing ToM. It uses the speech/belief distinction to measure factual-update retention after social exposure.
