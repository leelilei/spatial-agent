# AgentVerse: Facilitating Multi-Agent Collaboration and Exploring Emergent Behaviors in Agents

## Deep Read Status

## Why This Paper Matters For Telephone

AgentVerse broadens the agent-society context by presenting multi-agent collaboration and emergent behavior as a framework-level research object. It is useful background but not central evidence for our phenomenon.

## Core Question

The paper asks how to orchestrate multiple LLM agents so they can collaborate across tasks and exhibit group-level behaviors.

## Method / Evidence Base

It proposes a framework for agent recruitment, collaborative execution, and evaluation, inspired by human group dynamics.

## Core Claim / Result

The paper reinforces that LLM-agent groups are treated as societies whose interaction patterns may matter. It does not directly study factual transmission, correction, or belief persistence.

## Evidence We May Cite

- LLM-agent collaboration can be organized as a society-like workflow.
- Emergent behavior is an explicit motivation in multi-agent LLM research.
- The paper helps locate Telephone among agent-society studies rather than single-model benchmarks.

## Telephone Bridge

- Fidelity vs reach: collaboration success is not the same as faithful factual transmission.
- Speech vs belief: emergent conversation should be paired with hidden-state or post-hoc probes.
- Measurement / rigor: Telephone adds a controlled phenomenon metric to the agent-society setting.

## What We Add Beyond This Paper

Telephone does not propose a new orchestration framework. It supplies a stress test showing that social interaction can fail to maintain current truth even when the society is communicatively active.

## Draft-Ready Use Sentence

> AgentVerse treats LLM groups as orchestrated societies capable of collaboration and emergent behavior; Telephone asks a narrower reliability question inside such societies: what happens to factual updates as agents repeatedly communicate?
