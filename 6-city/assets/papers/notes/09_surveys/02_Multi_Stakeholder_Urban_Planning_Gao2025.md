# Simulating Multi-Stakeholder Decision-Making with Generative Agents in Urban Planning

## Why This Paper Matters For 6-city

This study shows a different urban use of generative agents: representing stakeholders in planning deliberation rather than residents physically executing daily intentions.

## Core Claim / Contribution

Eight AutoGen-based stakeholder agents, grounded in survey and interview personas, discuss a Kendall Square redevelopment proposal under six information and communication conditions.

## Evidence We May Cite

- Six prompt configurations vary communication, demographics, opinions, and life values.
- Each setup is repeated three times to inspect variability.
- Communication improves collective reasoning but may also amplify convergence or polarization.

## City Benchmark Bridge

- What it already measures: stakeholder dialogue, opinion development, and collective decision dynamics.
- What it does not measure: physical movement, time geography, resource constraints, or environment-verified task completion.
- How it informs CityAgency: supports a future deliberation track, but is not a CityIntent baseline.

## What We Add Beyond This Paper

CityAgency operationalizes urban agency through executable world transitions, while this paper studies discursive agency in planning participation.

## Draft-Ready Use Sentence

> Generative stakeholders can support exploratory planning deliberation, but their conversational outcomes do not establish physically executable resident behavior.

## Caveats / Follow-Up

- The local PDF is the open arXiv version of the later published article.
- Its small number of repeats limits strong claims about stochastic stability.
