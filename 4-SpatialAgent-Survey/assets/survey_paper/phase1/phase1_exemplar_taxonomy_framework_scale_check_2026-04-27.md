# Phase 1 Exemplar Taxonomy/Framework Scale Check

Date: 2026-04-27

Purpose: check how many articles, reviews, or frameworks supported the main taxonomy/framework in the scoping-review exemplars before using them as models for our own L0-L5 and `anchor_core / bridge_core` structure.

## Why This Matters

The question is not only whether an exemplar has a clean taxonomy. The more important question is how much evidence it used to justify that taxonomy or framework.

Our current widened-Core evidence map has:

- `29` paper-level widened-Core items
- `31` system/configuration rows
- `19` strict `anchor_core` rows
- `12` `bridge_core` rows

This is enough for a focused evidence map, but it is much smaller than broad health/education framework reviews. Therefore our L0-L5 taxonomy should be presented as a focused scoping taxonomy for a narrow emerging intersection, not as a mature field-wide framework.

## Reading For Our Corpus Size

The comparison gives a clear hierarchy:

1. Large framework/evidence-map reviews can support broad conceptual frameworks:
   - Tudor Car 2020: `30` existing frameworks.
   - Tudor Car 2022: `77` systematic reviews.
2. Broad LLM scoping reviews can support multi-dimensional coding schemes with around `100+` included studies:
   - Leiser 2025: `114` manuscripts.
3. Narrow emerging LLM-agent scoping reviews can work with a much smaller final corpus:
   - Silacci 2026: `10` studies.

Our widened-Core corpus of `29` papers / `31` rows sits between Silacci and Leiser:

- much larger than Silacci's `10` included studies;
- much smaller than Leiser's `114` included manuscripts;
- much smaller than Tudor Car-style framework reviews.

Therefore, our safest positioning is:

> This review offers a focused evidence-map taxonomy for an emerging intersection, not a field-wide mature framework.

## Consequence For L0-L5

Do not present L0-L5 as if it were validated like a mature competency framework.

Safe framing:

- "a coding taxonomy for agent-accessible spatial representation";
- "a scoping taxonomy derived from the mapped systems";
- "a working classification for organizing an emerging literature";
- "a framework for identifying gaps and guiding future empirical work."

Avoid:

- "a comprehensive framework of spatial intelligence in LLM agents";
- "the definitive taxonomy of spatial representation";
- "a validated framework";
- "a mature theory of spatial-social LLM systems."

## Consequence For Core Expansion

The widened-Core count is acceptable for a narrow scoping review if we keep the claim disciplined.

We do not need to chase the scale of Leiser or Tudor Car, because our topic is much narrower. However, we should avoid relying only on the strict `anchor_core = 17 papers / 19 rows` number when defending taxonomy coverage. The defensible count is the two-layer widened map:

- strict anchor evidence: `17` papers / `19` rows;
- widened evidence map: `29` papers / `31` rows.

The best comparison sentence is:

> Compared with broad LLM or digital-health scoping reviews that include tens to more than one hundred studies, our corpus is intentionally narrower; compared with recent scoping reviews of nascent LLM-agent applications, it is within a defensible range for mapping design patterns and reporting evidence gaps.

## Next Use In Our Plan

When we later write methodology or positioning, use the exemplars this way:

- Tudor Car 2022: model the evidence-map + framework + research-agenda structure, not the expected corpus size.
- Tudor Car 2020: model thematic synthesis of existing frameworks, but note that our source base is primary systems rather than existing frameworks.
- Leiser 2025: model coding dimensions and transparent reporting for heterogeneous LLM literature.
- Silacci 2026: model small-corpus, exploratory LLM-agent scoping-review rhetoric.
